Can three slices of a brain find its radiology report?

A retrieval baseline on MR-RATE, a large public set of brain MRI scans paired with radiology reports. It takes three axial slices per scan and ranks reports with a frozen medical vision-language model, MedSigLIP.

The pipeline samples three axial slices near 20%, 50% and 80% of the brain's height. Move them, or cut the brain open at any level.

    The brain shown here is the MNI ICBM152 average template, built from many healthy volunteers. It is not an MR-RATE scan and not a patient. Slice positions follow the same rule as the project code.

    From one scan to one ranking

    The model stays frozen. Nothing is trained or fine-tuned.

    1. ValidateCheck the joins between series, reports and splits, and confirm no patient crosses splits.
    2. SelectScreen for brain anatomy and keep one series per patient. FLAIR comes first, then T2w, then T1w.
    3. DownloadFetch only the selected study archives, under a fixed size cap.
    4. SliceReorient each volume to RAS axes and take three axial slices at 20%, 50% and 80%.
    5. EmbedEncode the slices with the MedSigLIP vision tower and average them. Encode the report impression with the text tower.
    6. RankRank every report in the split by cosine similarity. Report R@1, R@5 and MRR with patient bootstrap intervals.

    Where the project stands

    No retrieval result is reported yet.

    Download, validation and slice quality control are complete for the working batch. The MedSigLIP run on real data has not been done.

    This is a research baseline. It makes no clinical or diagnostic claim.
